⚠️ Foods Dogs Should NEVER Eat

The following foods can cause serious illness or death in dogs. Keep them out of reach at all times.

🍫
Chocolate
Dogs
Symptoms: Vomiting, diarrhoea, rapid breathing, muscle tremors, seizures, heart failure. Dark chocolate and baking chocolate are most dangerous.
🚨 Even small amounts can be fatal. Call your vet immediately.
🧅
Onions & Garlic
Dogs
Symptoms: Destroys red blood cells causing haemolytic anaemia. Signs include weakness, pale gums, reduced appetite, breathlessness. All forms dangerous: raw, cooked, powdered.
🚨 Toxicity is cumulative — repeated small doses are also dangerous.
🍇
Grapes & Raisins
Dogs
Symptoms: Acute kidney failure. Signs: vomiting, diarrhoea, lethargy, reduced urination. Even a single grape can be fatal in some dogs. Mechanism not fully understood.
🚨 No safe amount is known. Seek emergency vet care immediately.
🥜
Macadamia Nuts
Dogs
Symptoms: Weakness, hyperthermia, vomiting, tremors, inability to walk. Effects usually appear within 12 hours. Most cases resolve in 48 hours but vet care is essential.
🚨 Even 6 nuts can cause clinical signs in a small dog.
🍬
Xylitol (Artificial Sweetener)
Dogs
Symptoms: Rapid insulin release causing severe hypoglycaemia. Signs: vomiting, weakness, collapse, seizures, liver failure. Found in sugar-free gum, candy, peanut butter, baked goods, toothpaste.
🚨 Dangerously toxic — even tiny amounts require emergency care.
☕
Caffeine
Dogs
Symptoms: Restlessness, rapid breathing, heart palpitations, muscle tremors, seizures. Found in coffee, tea, energy drinks, chocolate, and some medications. No safe dose exists for dogs.
🚨 Toxic to the heart and nervous system. Treat as emergency.
🍷
Alcohol
Dogs
Symptoms: Vomiting, disorientation, dangerous drops in blood sugar/pressure/temperature, breathing difficulties, seizures, coma. Even beer, wine, or bread dough (which ferments) is dangerous.
🚨 There is no safe amount of alcohol for dogs. Emergency vet required.
🥑
Avocado
Dogs
Symptoms: The high fat content may cause stomach upset or pancreatitis in dogs. The pit is also a choking hazard.
🚨 Keep avocado and its pit away from dogs.
Important Note: This list is not exhaustive. When in doubt about a new food, consult your veterinarian before feeding it to your dog. If you believe your dog has ingested a toxic substance, contact the Pet Poison Helpline (+1-855-764-7661) or your nearest emergency vet immediately.

🍳 Healthy Homemade Recipes

Simple, vet-safe recipe ideas for an occasional homemade treat. Always consult your vet for a fully balanced diet plan.

Disclaimer: These recipes are intended as occasional supplemental meals or treats, not as complete diet replacements. Homemade diets require careful formulation to ensure nutritional balance. Always consult a veterinary nutritionist or your vet before transitioning to a homemade diet. Individual pets may have allergies or sensitivities.
🍗
Chicken & Rice Recovery Meal
🐶 Dogs
Ingredients
  • 1 cup plain boneless chicken breast (no seasoning)
  • 2 cups plain white rice (cooked)
  • 1 cup low-sodium chicken broth (no onion/garlic)
  • ½ cup carrots (cooked, mashed)
Instructions
  • Boil chicken until fully cooked. Shred finely.
  • Cook rice in broth for extra flavour (without additives).
  • Mix together with mashed carrots and cool completely.
  • Serve at room temperature, refrigerate remainder.
Benefits
  • Bland and easy to digest — ideal during stomach upsets
  • Lean protein supports muscle recovery
  • Plain carbohydrates soothe the gut
⚠️ Not a complete diet. Use as a temporary meal during recovery or as an occasional supplement. Consult your vet before use.

📅 Feeding Schedule Guide

How often to feed based on your dog's age. Portion sizes vary by breed and weight — consult your vet or the food packaging for specific amounts.

🐶 Dog Feeding Schedule
Age / Stage Meals Per Day Food Type Notes
Puppy (0–3 weeks)Mother's milk / every 2 hrsMother's milk or puppy formulaDo not separate from mother
Puppy (3–6 weeks)4–5 times dailyBegin softened puppy kibble + mother's milkWeaning period — gradual introduction
Puppy (6–12 weeks)4 times dailyHigh-quality puppy kibbleSmall, frequent meals for developing gut
Puppy (3–6 months)3 times dailyPuppy formula kibbleMonitor for consistent growth
Puppy (6–12 months)2–3 times dailyPuppy or transitional adult foodLarge breeds need puppy food longer
Adult (1–7 years)2 times dailyComplete adult dog foodMorning and evening feeding recommended
Senior (7+ years)2 times dailySenior / joint-support dog foodReduced calories; more fibre for digestion
Pregnant/Nursing3–4 times dailyPuppy formula or high-calorie adult foodIncreased caloric needs; vet guidance required
Pro Tips:
  • Always provide fresh, clean water alongside every meal.
  • Use measuring cups to avoid overfeeding — obesity is one of the most common preventable pet health problems.
  • Transition between foods gradually over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset: 25% new / 75% old → 50/50 → 75% new / 25% old → 100% new.
  • Feed at consistent times each day to establish healthy digestive rhythms.
