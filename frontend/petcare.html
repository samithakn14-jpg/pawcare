Pet Health & Care

Everything you need to know to keep your furry, feathered, or fluffy companion healthy and happy.

⚠️

Educational Purposes Only: This information is for general guidance only. For serious or emergency conditions, contact a qualified veterinarian immediately. Never delay seeking professional veterinary advice based on information found here.

Basic Pet Care

Essential daily and weekly routines to keep your pet in top condition.

🛁
Grooming

Regular grooming keeps your pet's coat healthy and reduces shedding. Brush short-haired dogs weekly, long-haired dogs daily. Use a slicker brush or grooming mitt suited to the coat type. Check for tangles, mats, fleas, or skin irritation. Trim nails every 3–4 weeks — overgrown nails cause pain and posture problems. Use pet-specific nail clippers and have styptic powder on hand in case of bleeding.

🚿
Bathing

Most dogs benefit from a bath every 4–6 weeks. Always use pH-balanced, dog-safe shampoo — human shampoos disrupt your dog's skin barrier. Use lukewarm water and thoroughly rinse to prevent skin irritation. Dry with a towel and keep your dog warm afterward. Never get water directly into the ears — use cotton balls as a barrier during baths.

🏃
Exercise

Dogs need 30–120 minutes of exercise daily depending on breed and age. High-energy breeds (Labradors, Border Collies) require vigorous daily activity; smaller breeds need shorter walks. Adjust activity to your dog's age and health, and use puzzle feeders and training games for mental stimulation. Exercise helps prevent obesity, anxiety, and destructive behaviour.

🧹
Hygiene

Clean your dog's ears as advised by your vet using a vet-approved ear cleaner and cotton balls — never insert anything into the ear canal. Check for redness, odour, or dark discharge (signs of infection). Wipe paw pads after outdoor walks to remove debris, chemicals, or allergens. Clean around the eyes daily with a damp cloth to remove discharge.

🦷
Dental Care

Brush your dog's teeth 2–3 times per week using dog-specific toothpaste (never human toothpaste — xylitol is toxic). Offer dental treats, chews, and water additives approved by the Veterinary Oral Health Council (VOHC). Signs of dental disease include bad breath, difficulty eating, pawing at the mouth, drooling, and red or swollen gums. Ask your vet whether professional dental cleaning is recommended.

🛌
Sleep & Rest

Adult dogs sleep 12–14 hours per day; puppies and seniors may sleep up to 18–20 hours. Provide a comfortable, draft-free sleeping area with appropriate bedding. Respect your dog's rest — waking them abruptly can cause stress or anxiety. Sudden changes in sleep patterns may signal illness; consult your vet.

Internal parasites (worms) are common in dogs and can affect both dog and human health. Regular deworming is a key part of preventive care.

How Often Should You Deworm?
  • Puppies: Follow your veterinarian's schedule, often every 2 weeks from 2 weeks of age until 12 weeks, then monthly until 6 months.
  • Adult dogs: Deworming frequency depends on lifestyle, local parasite risks, and test results. Ask your veterinarian for a schedule.
  • Pregnant/Nursing Females: As directed by your vet (usually before and after birth).

🚨 Emergency First Aid

What to do in a crisis — while you get your pet to a veterinarian as quickly as possible.

🩹
Injury / Bleeding

Stay calm — your pet will sense your stress. Restrain gently to prevent biting from pain.

  • Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth or bandage.
  • Do not remove the cloth if it becomes soaked — add more on top.
  • Keep the wound elevated if possible.
  • Do not apply a tourniquet unless directed by a vet.
  • Transport to emergency vet immediately.
🚨

EMERGENCY: Contact a veterinarian immediately. This information is first-aid guidance only.

☢️
Poisoning / Toxin Ingestion

If you suspect your pet has eaten something toxic, act immediately.

  • Do NOT induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by a vet or poison hotline.
  • Identify the substance if possible (keep the packaging).
  • Note the time of ingestion and amount consumed.
  • Call the Pet Poison Helpline or your emergency vet at once.
  • Do not give food, water, or home remedies.
🚨

EMERGENCY: Contact a veterinarian immediately. This information is first-aid guidance only.

😫
Breathing Difficulty

Laboured breathing, blue or grey gums, choking, or collapse is a life-threatening emergency.

  • Keep your pet calm and minimize exertion.
  • Check the mouth carefully for visible obstructions — remove only if easily accessible.
  • Do not muzzle a pet struggling to breathe.
  • Keep the airway clear and head slightly extended.
  • Get to an emergency vet immediately — do not wait.
🚨

EMERGENCY: Contact a veterinarian immediately. This information is first-aid guidance only.

🤢
Severe Vomiting / Diarrhoea

Occasional vomiting can be normal, but persistent or severe episodes require attention.

  • Withhold food for 1–2 hours to let the stomach settle.
  • Offer small amounts of water to prevent dehydration.
  • Monitor for blood in vomit or stool.
  • Watch for signs of dehydration: sunken eyes, dry gums, lethargy.
  • See a vet if vomiting occurs more than 2–3 times, or persists beyond 24 hours.
🚨

EMERGENCY: Contact a veterinarian immediately. This information is first-aid guidance only.

🥵
Heat Stroke

Signs include excessive panting, drooling, bright red gums, weakness, and collapse. Act fast.

  • Move your pet immediately to a cool, shaded area.
  • Apply cool (not cold/icy) water to paws, armpits, and groin.
  • Use a fan to help evaporative cooling.
  • Offer small sips of cool water if conscious.
  • Do not cover with a wet towel — this traps heat.
  • Call your vet immediately and transport with windows open.
🚨

EMERGENCY: Contact a veterinarian immediately. This information is first-aid guidance only.

⚡
Seizures

Seizures can look alarming. Your first job is to keep your pet and yourself safe.

  • Do NOT put your hands near the mouth — pets cannot swallow their tongue, but they can bite.
  • Clear the surrounding area of hard objects.
  • Time the seizure — if it lasts more than 5 minutes, go to the emergency vet now.
  • Keep the area dark and quiet to reduce stimulation.
  • After it stops, keep your pet warm and calm.
  • All first-time seizures require immediate veterinary evaluation.
🚨

EMERGENCY: Contact a veterinarian immediately. This information is first-aid guidance only.

Care Tips by Species

Every animal has unique needs — find species-specific guidance below.

🦮
Socialisation

Expose puppies to diverse people, animals, sounds, and environments between 3–14 weeks of age. Well-socialised dogs are calmer and less fearful throughout life.

🥣
Feeding

Feed age-appropriate, high-quality dog food. Puppies need 3–4 meals per day; adults 2 meals. Avoid table scraps — many human foods are toxic to dogs.

🎾
Mental Stimulation

Use puzzle feeders, training sessions, and nose-work games to keep dogs mentally sharp. A mentally tired dog is a well-behaved dog.

🏥
Spay / Neuter

Spaying or neutering helps prevent unwanted litters, reduces cancer risks, and can reduce aggression. Discuss the ideal timing with your vet (usually 6–12 months).

🛡️
Parasite Prevention

Use monthly flea, tick, and heartworm preventives year-round. Check for ticks after outdoor activities, especially in wooded areas.

🏡
Safe Environment

Dog-proof your home: secure trash, lock away toxic substances, remove small objects that could be swallowed, and use pet-safe cleaning products.
