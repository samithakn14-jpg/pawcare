Pet Medicine Information

General medicine information for pet owners. Species and individual health matter: always consult a veterinarian before administering medicine.

⚠️

IMPORTANT: Read Before Continuing

Never give medicine to your dog without proper veterinary advice.

  • Do not self-diagnose your dog's condition or self-administer prescription or over-the-counter medications without veterinary guidance.
  • Many human medications are highly toxic and potentially lethal to dogs — including common drugs like paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen, and aspirin.
  • This page provides educational information only about general medication categories. It does not constitute veterinary advice or a recommendation for treatment.
  • Always consult a licensed, qualified veterinarian for any diagnosis, dosing, or treatment plan for your dog.
  • If your dog is showing signs of illness or you suspect poisoning, contact your vet or an emergency animal hospital immediately.

Common Dog Health Conditions

Educational overview of frequent health issues in dogs and guidance on when professional care is needed.

Skin issues are among the most common reasons pets visit the vet. They can range from minor irritation to serious systemic disease.

🦟
Flea Allergy Dermatitis (FAD)

An allergic reaction to flea saliva causing intense itching, hair loss, and skin inflammation. Even a single flea bite can trigger a severe reaction in sensitive pets.

  • Signs: Excessive scratching, especially at the base of the tail and inner thighs
  • Hot spots (acute moist dermatitis) may develop from persistent scratching
  • Year-round flea prevention is the most effective treatment
🌾
Environmental & Food Allergies

Pets can develop allergies to pollen, mould, dust mites, or specific food proteins. Allergies are one of the most complex conditions to diagnose in veterinary medicine.

  • Signs: Chronic itching, recurring ear infections, paw licking, facial rubbing
  • Food trials (elimination diets) lasting 8–12 weeks are required to diagnose food allergies
  • Environmental allergies may require allergy testing and immunotherapy
🔬
Ringworm (Fungal Infection)

Despite the name, ringworm is a contagious fungal infection, not a worm. It causes circular patches of hair loss and can be transmitted to humans (zoonotic).

  • Signs: Circular bald patches, scaly skin, broken hairs
  • Diagnosed via UV lamp (Wood's lamp), culture, or PCR test
  • Highly contagious — isolate affected pets and disinfect environment
👨‍⚕️ Consult your veterinarian for proper diagnosis and treatment. Skin conditions often require prescription antifungals, antibiotics, antiparasitics, or immunosuppressants.

Gastrointestinal problems are extremely common in pets and vary from mild dietary upset to serious life-threatening conditions.

🤢
Acute Gastroenteritis

Sudden-onset vomiting and diarrhoea, often caused by dietary indiscretion (eating something unusual), sudden diet changes, or minor infections.

  • Signs: Vomiting, loose/watery stools, mild lethargy, reduced appetite
  • Most mild cases resolve with 24-hour food rest and bland diet
  • Monitor for blood in vomit or stool, dehydration, or worsening symptoms
🔄
Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

Chronic inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract. A complex condition requiring thorough investigation and long-term management.

  • Signs: Chronic/recurrent vomiting or diarrhoea, weight loss, poor coat condition
  • Diagnosis requires bloodwork, imaging, and often intestinal biopsy
  • Managed with prescription diets, anti-inflammatory medications, or immunosuppressants
🚨
Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus (GDV / Bloat)

Life-threatening emergency — the stomach fills with gas and rotates, cutting off blood supply. Most common in large, deep-chested breeds (Great Danes, German Shepherds, etc.).

  • Signs: Distended/hard abdomen, unproductive retching, restlessness, rapid deterioration
  • Requires immediate emergency surgery — death can occur within hours
  • Do NOT wait — go to an emergency vet the moment these symptoms appear
👨‍⚕️ Consult your veterinarian for proper diagnosis and treatment. Never withhold veterinary care hoping digestive symptoms will resolve on their own.

Respiratory conditions range from mild upper respiratory infections to serious heart and lung disease. Any difficulty breathing warrants urgent veterinary attention.

🤧
Upper Respiratory Infection (URI)

Kennel cough (Bordetella) is a contagious respiratory infection that can spread quickly anywhere dogs gather, including boarding facilities and parks.

  • Signs: Sneezing, nasal discharge, eye discharge, mild fever, reduced appetite
  • Signs can include a dry, honking cough, sneezing, nasal discharge, and reduced appetite
  • Puppies, seniors, and immunocompromised dogs are at higher risk of complications
❤️
Heart Disease & Congestive Heart Failure

Common in older dogs and certain breeds (Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, Dachshunds). Causes fluid accumulation in and around the lungs.

  • Signs: Persistent cough (especially at night), exercise intolerance, rapid breathing, distended abdomen
  • Diagnosed via X-rays, echocardiogram, and blood tests
  • Managed with diuretics, ACE inhibitors, and cardiac medications — lifelong treatment
👨‍⚕️ Consult your veterinarian for proper diagnosis and treatment. Breathing difficulties are a medical emergency — never wait to see if they improve.

Musculoskeletal conditions significantly impact a pet's quality of life. Early detection and management can slow progression and maintain mobility.

🚶
Osteoarthritis (OA)

Osteoarthritis causes degeneration of joint cartilage, leading to chronic pain and reduced mobility. It is common in senior dogs and certain breeds.

  • Signs: Stiffness after rest, reluctance to jump or climb stairs, licking/biting at joints, behaviour changes, reduced activity
  • Managed with weight control, joint supplements, physiotherapy, NSAIDs, and newer medications
🐕
Hip Dysplasia

Abnormal development of the hip joint — most common in large and giant breed dogs (German Shepherds, Labradors, Golden Retrievers). Has a strong genetic component.

  • Signs: Bunny-hopping gait, reluctance to exercise, lameness, loss of thigh muscle mass
  • Diagnosed via X-rays
  • Treatment ranges from weight management and physiotherapy to surgical correction in severe cases
🐩
Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD)

Herniation of spinal discs causing spinal cord compression. Common in Dachshunds, Beagles, Cocker Spaniels, and other chondrodystrophic breeds.

  • Signs: Neck/back pain, hunched posture, wobbly gait, reluctance to move, paralysis in severe cases
  • Emergency: Sudden paralysis or inability to urinate requires immediate veterinary assessment
  • Treatment: Conservative management (cage rest + medication) or surgery depending on severity
👨‍⚕️ Consult your veterinarian for proper diagnosis and treatment. Joint conditions require X-rays, physical examination, and often specialist referral for accurate management.

💉 Understanding Pet Vaccines

Educational information about how vaccines work and why they are essential for your dog's health. This is not a dosage guide — always follow your vet's advice.

Vaccines are one of the most important tools in preventive veterinary medicine. They train your dog's immune system to recognise and fight specific pathogens without causing the actual disease. Recommended vaccines depend on age, lifestyle, and geographic location — work with your vet to create a personalised vaccination schedule.
🛡️
Core vs Non-Core Vaccines

Core vaccines are recommended for dogs regardless of lifestyle and protect against widespread or severe diseases. Examples include rabies and DHPP.

Non-core vaccines are recommended based on individual risk factors such as geographic location, lifestyle, and exposure to other dogs. Examples include leptospirosis and Bordetella.

📅
Puppy Vaccine Series

Puppies require a series of vaccines given weeks apart to build sufficient immunity. Maternal antibodies can interfere with vaccine response, so doses are spaced as those antibodies wane. Missing doses or spacing them incorrectly can leave gaps in protection. Always complete the full series as directed by your vet.

🔄
Booster Vaccines

Immunity from vaccines does not always last a lifetime. Boosters are given to maintain protection — frequency depends on the vaccine type (some are annual, others every 3 years). Titre testing is available for some vaccines to measure current immunity levels, which can guide decisions about booster timing. Never skip boosters without discussing with your vet.

⚡
Possible Side Effects

Most dogs experience no or minimal side effects. Mild, temporary reactions are normal and indicate the immune system is responding: slight soreness at injection site, mild lethargy for 24–48 hours, reduced appetite. Rare severe reactions (anaphylaxis) can include facial swelling, vomiting, difficulty breathing, or collapse — seek emergency care if these occur within a few hours of vaccination.

📋
Keeping Vaccination Records

Always keep a written or digital record of all vaccines administered, including the date, product name, batch number, and due date for the next dose. Most countries require proof of up-to-date rabies vaccination for travel. Log your pet's vaccination history securely on your PetCare Connect Dashboard and set reminders for upcoming boosters.
